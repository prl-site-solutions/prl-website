Why Energy-from-Waste is different

An energy-from-waste plant burns residual waste to generate electricity, and sometimes heat. Building one is a long, heavy industrial project. First come deep, thick reinforced concrete structures for the waste bunker and boiler house, then a large mechanical, piping and electrical installation, all under strict HSE and quality control.

That mix is why EfW contractors struggle to staff it through general agencies. The steel fixers need heavy RC experience, not light mesh. The supervisors need to have run a process site. The QA/QC team has to manage weld traceability across thousands of joints. EfW is one of our core specialisms.

Recent EfW and industrial roles we've recruited for include civils foremen and steel fixers at Ince in Cheshire; site supervisors, HSE advisors and fire marshals in Pembrokeshire; and plater fabricators on a new build at Bridgwater in Somerset.

Frequently asked questions

What is an energy-from-waste (EfW) plant?

An EfW plant burns non-recyclable household and commercial waste to generate electricity, and in some cases heat. Building one involves heavy civils (waste bunker, tipping hall, boiler house), followed by mechanical, piping, and electrical and instrumentation installation, typically over several years.

Which EfW roles do you recruit for?

Civils foremen, groundworkers and steel fixers; platers, fabricators and coded welders; electricians; site supervisors; HSE advisors and fire marshals; and piping and welding QA/QC inspectors.

Do your workers have EfW experience?

For supervisory, HSE and QA/QC roles we put forward people with EfW, power or process experience. For trades we match on the type of work: heavy RC for steel fixers, coded pipe welding for welders, and so on.

Where are your EfW placements?

Wherever the project is. Recent EfW and industrial roles have been at Ince in Cheshire, in Pembrokeshire, at Deeside and at Bridgwater in Somerset, and we place nationwide.
